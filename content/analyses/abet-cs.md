> *Working draft for faculty review. Checks the redesigned B.S. in Computer Science against ABET's computer-science program criteria. The binding question: does the **required** path (common core + capstone + whatever stays required) independently satisfy ABET, with former-required 500-levels as elective depth? CIS numbers are actual K-State courses.*
>
> Every claim below has been checked against real course content, following the same method used for the threads/lenses/assessment-chapter passes: confirm what's actually drafted, flag what's confirmed design intent but undrafted, and correct what turns out not to hold.

## Why this is the binding analysis

The project's scope escalated from "replace sub-500 CIS" to "restructure the entire required CS core," with courses like CIS 560 and 575 migrating to elective status (for the general CS degree — real specialization degree maps already built, `cybersecurity.md` and `ai-systems.md`, require them). ABET is the constraint that makes this safe or not: the **required** path must satisfy every CS criterion on its own, with electives providing genuine depth rather than load-bearing coverage. This analysis maps the criteria against the core and identifies exactly which gaps force a course to remain required.

| Statistics | Same STAT 410, same caveat | **Covered by confirmed course placement, not yet by verifiable content** |
| Rigor ≥ introductory calculus | Year-2 calculus (MATH 220, Y1 Spring) + the Year-2 linear-algebra elective (MATH 515 or 551, both confirmed real courses in `cybersecurity.md`/`ai-systems.md`'s degree maps) sit within the 15-SCH block | **Met at the block level** — see tension below |

**The rigor tension.** The foundation statistics is deliberately *non-calculus*. ABET wants the math/stats block to carry calculus-level rigor. The resolution: the 15-SCH block *includes* the calculus and linear-algebra courses, so the **block** clears the rigor bar even though STAT 410 itself is conceptual. Two consequences to confirm: (1) calculus must be counted within the 15 SCH, and (2) STAT 410's actual content, once drafted, needs to be checked against what "probability" and "statistics" require here — it can't be verified yet.

## The three real gaps — and how they decide required vs. elective

The value of this analysis: the ABET gaps **determine which 500-level courses must stay required**. Courses whose *foundations* the core already covers can become electives; courses that fill a core gap must stay required.

1. **Computer science theory (automata, computability, formal languages) — re-examined 2026-08-04.** A confirmed, dated (2026-07-14) plan exists to distribute this across CIS 115/200/220/225/300/301/505/560/575, but checking every one of those courses' real drafted pages found none of it actually written in yet. **Implication, revised:** this is a *conditional* gap. If the distributed plan gets drafted into those courses' actual content, no new required course is needed — the plan is sound and doesn't require CIS 570/770 or similar. If it doesn't get drafted (or faculty don't want to rely on content spread this thin across 8 courses), a required formal-theory course is the fallback. This needs a decision, not just tracking: commit to drafting the distributed plan, or add a required theory course now.
2. **General-purpose language, substantial coverage — re-examined 2026-08-04.** Less absent than the old "language-agnostic" framing suggested: CIS 200 → CIS 300 give two consecutive courses (~6 credits) in one (still-undecided) OO language. But that's short of "substantial... in at least one general-purpose language" on its own, and CIS 116's Python doesn't extend it (different language). **Implication — one of:** (a) a language course stays required in the degree; (b) CIS 116 is brought into the same language as CIS 200/300 to make it three consecutive courses; (c) the certificate model is restructured so a certificate counts toward degree credit for ABET. Also flagged: `cybersecurity.md` and `ai-systems.md` currently claim this requirement is already met across three courses — that claim is inconsistent with `cs.md`'s own language decisions and needs correcting in those files too.
3. **Operating systems / parallel & distributed exposure — corrected 2026-08-04, no longer a core-content gap but no longer a solved-by-the-core story either.** The deep formalization this item used to cite as woven into the core was never actually built there. It's covered instead by CIS 450 and the already-required Systems Elective (CIS 520/525/625) — real coverage, just not core coverage. The intent to eventually place some of this grounding within the core remains open (`spiral-threads.md`), pending a decision on where and how deep. **Note:** `cybersecurity.md`'s own degree map hard-codes CIS 525 alone (not the flexible Systems Elective choice `ai-systems.md` uses) — that specific degree's OS-exposure coverage needs its own direct check, flagged in `cybersecurity.md`'s faculty concerns.

By contrast, the courses the restructure *wanted* to make elective are safe to, for the general CS degree: **CIS 560 (databases)** and **CIS 575 (algorithm analysis)** — the core covers their foundations (CIS 260 databases; CIS 300 algorithms), so they become legitimate elective depth rather than required coverage. (Both are required, not elective, in the Cybersecurity and AI Systems specializations specifically — that's a specialization-depth choice, not an ABET-required-coverage one.)

## Open questions

1. **CS theory — sharper now, still open.** Commit to drafting the distributed plan into CIS 115/200/220/225/300/301/505/560/575's real content, or add a required theory course as a fallback? This is no longer "does a plan exist" (it does) but "will it get executed," which is course-designer/faculty-scheduling territory.
2. **Language coverage.** Which resolution — required language course, extending CIS 116 into the same language as CIS 200/300, or certificates restructured to count? Also: correct `cybersecurity.md`/`ai-systems.md`'s overclaim on this point once a resolution is chosen, so all three documents agree.
3. **Operating systems / parallel & distributed — corrected, not resolved.** Coverage rests on CIS 450 and the required Systems Elective, not core content. Confirm this is an acceptable long-term answer, and separately confirm whether Cybersecurity's CIS-525-only Year 4 sequence (vs. AI Systems' flexible Systems Elective choice) still clears this bar on its own.
4. **"Substantial" thresholds.** Faculty judgment on whether the now-confirmed real algorithms/architecture coverage (CIS 300, CIS 450) clears "substantial"/"exposure" without the former depth courses required.
5. **Math rigor accounting.** Confirm calculus counts within the 15-SCH math/stats block, and — new — confirm STAT 410's eventual real content actually covers probability and statistics adequately once it's drafted; right now this rests on a confirmed course placement with zero verifiable content.
6. **New, 2026-08-04 — CIS 501/CIS 505's required (not elective) status changes the required-vs-elective accounting** used throughout this document's framing. Worth a pass confirming this doesn't shift the credit-hour math for the general CS degree's own path.

## Bottom line

The redesigned core satisfies the **majority** of ABET CS criteria, several of them strongly and — after this rewrite — verifiably against real, drafted course content rather than design intent alone (programming-language concepts, software development, abstraction, information management, computer architecture, networking). The accreditation work is now concentrated in **two real gaps** — general-purpose-language depth, and CS theory (which, unusually, has a confirmed plan but zero executed content) — plus one clarified non-gap: operating-systems/parallel-distributed exposure is **covered but not by the core**, resting on CIS 450 and the required Systems Elective instead, with the intent to eventually place some of that grounding within the core still explicitly open. CIS 501 and CIS 505 are **leaning required, not elective**, correcting this document's own earlier framing and matching what the real specialization degree maps already require. The one genuine philosophical decision forced by ABET remains the **general-purpose-language requirement**, now sharper: the core provides two courses of real depth (CIS 200/300), not zero, but that's still short of "substantial," and it must be resolved explicitly — including correcting the overclaim currently sitting in `cybersecurity.md` and `ai-systems.md`.
