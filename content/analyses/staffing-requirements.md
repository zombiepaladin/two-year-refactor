> *Working draft for faculty review. Analyzes teaching-load requirements for the proposed two-year CS core under three offering-frequency models, anchored to the K-State faculty workload policy (FHXY). Enrollment figures assume 60–100 incoming students per year. Core course codes are placeholders.*

## Policy baseline

Kansas State University Faculty Handbook policy FHXY establishes that the representative nine-month workload for tenure-track faculty is 40% teaching / 40% research / 20% service, with 40% teaching translating to approximately **12 section credit hours (SCH) per academic year**. The conversion scales proportionally:

| Teaching allocation | Annual SCH capacity |
|---|---|
| 60% | 18 SCH/yr |
| 70% | 21 SCH/yr |
| 75% | 22.5 SCH/yr |
| 80% | 24 SCH/yr |

Teaching-track faculty with reduced or eliminated research obligations carry higher teaching allocations. The 75–80% range represents the practical ceiling for full-time teaching-track positions under the policy.

## Core teaching load

The proposed core contains **22 CS courses**, each carrying **1 credit hour**. Eight co-designed MATH and STAT courses are excluded — those are staffed by their home departments. Under the SCH metric, each section offered generates 1 SCH of teaching load.

In steady state, two cohorts are active simultaneously: Year 1 students in Blocks 1–4, Year 2 students in Blocks 5–8. All 22 CS courses must therefore be staffed every academic year.

**Section count is the primary enrollment driver.** With a section cap of approximately 30 students:

| Entering cohort size | Sections needed per course per offering |
|---|---|
| ~60 students | 2 sections |
| ~90 students | 3 sections |
| ~100 students | 3–4 sections |

## Annual SCH demand by scenario

Total annual SCH = 22 courses × offerings per year × sections per course.

Three offering-frequency models are analyzed:

- **S1 — Once per year:** Each course runs in its designated 8-week block once annually. A student who misses a course must wait until the following year.
- **S2 — Every semester:** Each course is offered once in fall and once in spring. Students have a within-year recovery window.
- **S3 — Every 8-week block:** All 22 courses are offered in every 8-week block (4 times per year). This is the minimum offering frequency to support a true competency-based model where students can advance or recover on their own schedule.

| Scenario | Offerings/yr | ~60 students (2 sections) | ~90 students (3 sections) | ~100 students (4 sections) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| S1: Once per year | 1 | 44 SCH | 66 SCH | 88 SCH |
| S2: Every semester | 2 | 88 SCH | 132 SCH | 176 SCH |
| S3: Every 8-week block | 4 | 176 SCH | 264 SCH | 352 SCH |

## Structural observations

### The SCH metric understates preparation burden

All 22 core courses carry 1 credit, so each section counts as 1 SCH. However, preparation burden for a unique 1-credit course is not proportionally less than for a 3-credit course. A faculty member teaching 12 distinct 1-credit core courses per year carries the same SCH count as someone teaching one 3-credit course four times — but with twelve separate course preparations. This is a meaningful workload inequity that the SCH metric does not capture.

Any workload agreement for this curriculum should negotiate a preparation-adjusted equivalency, particularly for the first time a course is delivered. This is especially acute in Scenario 3, where the full course catalog must be prepared and maintained concurrently.

### S3 redistributes enrollment, it does not reduce total work

In a true CBE model (S3), enrollment is spread across four offerings per year instead of one or two. For 90 incoming students spread across four blocks, each offering has approximately 23 students — which may reduce the sections-per-offering count. However, total student-course contacts remain the same, so total SCH demanded scales directly with offering frequency. S3 decreases section size but increases offering count proportionally; there is no net reduction in faculty load.

### Mixed-load contributions offset but do not replace dedicated positions

Faculty carrying partial core assignments, instructors assigned to specific content areas, and graduate teaching assistants can all contribute SCH toward the core. These partial contributions reduce the number of dedicated positions needed but introduce scheduling fragility. In particular, graduate teaching assistants should be treated as variable rather than fixed capacity — PhD-program timelines create turnover on two- to three-year cycles, and a GRA departure mid-program disrupts course continuity in a tightly sequenced curriculum.

## Recommendations

### S1 — Launch posture (3–4 positions)

S1 is viable with **3 dedicated teaching-track faculty at 75–80% teaching** for the projected enrollment range. A fourth position is warranted if enrollment reaches 100 students or if the curriculum coordinator role is carried by one of the core faculty.

This is the lowest-risk starting configuration. It allows the department to validate the block structure, competency-assessment model, and spiral pacing with a real cohort before committing to higher offering frequencies. Surplus capacity at S1 (roughly 40–70 SCH/year above minimum demand) can absorb partial contributions from research-track faculty, upper-division coordination, and curriculum revision work without restructuring the teaching pool.

**Enrollment trigger for a fourth hire: ~80 students** (before strain appears, not in response to it).

### S2 — Scaled operation (4–8 positions)

S2 requires **4 dedicated positions at 75–80% for 60 students, scaling to 6–8 positions at 90–100 students**. This is appropriate as a second-phase commitment once the curriculum has been validated through at least two complete cohort cycles and enrollment has stabilized.

The department should define an explicit enrollment threshold at which S2 becomes operationally justified — approximately 70–75 students, where the within-semester recovery window serves enough students to offset the doubled offering cost. Below that threshold, S1 with targeted repeat-section provisions is more efficient.

### S3 — Full CBE support (8–15 positions)

S3 requires **8–15 dedicated teaching-track faculty** across the projected enrollment range — a staffing commitment roughly 3–4× larger than S1. This is not viable as a launch posture and should only be planned if the department has secured institutional enrollment guarantees and a sustained budget commitment for a large teaching faculty.

A hybrid path is worth exploring: S3 delivery supported by asynchronous or technology-mediated materials could allow a single course preparation to serve multiple concurrent sections with reduced per-section faculty contact. This would compress the headcount requirement but introduces instructional-design complexity and requires a different model for competency verification than the current assessment framework assumes.

### Recommended trajectory

| Phase | Timeline | Scenario | Positions |
|---|---|---|---|
| Launch | Year 1–2 | S1 | 3 (add 4th if enrollment > 80) |
| Validation | Year 2–4 | S1 or S2 at low enrollment | 3–4 |
| Scale | Year 4+ | S2 | 4–8 depending on enrollment |
| Full CBE | TBD | S3 | 8–15; requires separate resource plan |
