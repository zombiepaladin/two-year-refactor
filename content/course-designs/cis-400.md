## Course Description

A study of concepts and techniques used to produce quality software programs. Object-oriented concepts, models, execution environments, design and testing techniques. Extensive application of these concepts and techniques to the development of non-trivial software programs.

## Credit Hours

3 credits

## Course Overview

Not yet drafted as a full syllabus, but the redesign direction for this course is the most extensively confirmed of any course in the core — see `resources/reference/degree-maps/cs.md`'s CIS 400 entry in full. Summary: this becomes a **capstone of the core** — a substantial, deliberately ambiguous-requirements team project (small teams, rotating scope by cohort) building toward the historical archive real-world asset (see `content/pedagogy/signature-applications/historical-archive.md`). It replaces a prior solo, well-defined-spec point-of-sale project, retired because AI could complete it milestone-by-milestone without the student demonstrating understanding. Assessment leans on defended/oral components (live walkthrough, architecture defense). This course is also the confirmed team-project host for the Sociotechnical Structure thread and the individual/collective Professional Practices split, and absorbs testing (formalized here, though woven throughout earlier courses), deployment/operations content, and the Design Review signature assessment — see the thread files in `content/core-design/threads/` for the full cross-referencing.
