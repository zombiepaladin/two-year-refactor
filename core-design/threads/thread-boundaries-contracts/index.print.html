Boundaries & Contracts

Working draft for faculty review. A spiral deepens through escalating passes across the two-year core. This is the spiral (meta-thread); one of eight spirals (with three lenses and one bounded practice — twelve threads total, plus two cross-cutting norms).

In one line: A promise about behavior at a boundary, independent of what’s hidden behind it — escalating by the kind of boundary the contract spans.

Character

Both a spiral and a meta-thread: it names the common structure beneath ADTs, APIs, schemas, trust boundaries, versioning, and verification. Arguably the strongest expression of the program’s systems-thinking ethos — and the thread that absorbed the “boundaries and contracts” idea the now-retired APIs & Networked Systems spiral also carried (confirmed sufficient on its own, 2026-07-15; see resources/design-log.md).

Passes across the two years

Course What’s new at this pass
CIS 200 / CIS 300 (Y1 Spring / Y2 Fall) Boundary = an interface within one program: class encapsulation (CIS 200’s OOP introduction) and ADT contracts (CIS 300) — names the informal pre/post-condition reasoning seeded earlier.
CIS 220 / CIS 300 / CIS 320 (Y1 Spring / Y2 Fall) Boundary becomes a process/network boundary: an API contract (request → response), the other side unseen — the CIS 300 (server) / CIS 320 (client) pairing, with CIS 220 carrying the structural-boundary depth. Partially confirmed (2026-08-03): CIS 220’s part is well-supported — its real content explicitly names “origins as a security/process boundary.” CIS 300/320’s API-contract specifics remain unconfirmed for the reasons already logged in the Computational Models review: the client/server vehicle isn’t spelled out in cis-300.md (confirmed intentional, see its “Proposed Changes”), and CIS 320 has no drafted schedule yet.
CIS 225 (Y2 Fall) — Foundations of Computer Networks Boundary becomes a protocol layer: the OSI 7-layer model as a stack of contracts, each layer promising an interface to the one above regardless of what’s underneath — a protocol is a contract about message format. Confirmed directly by CIS 225’s real SLOs (“explain the 7-level OSI model, and discuss where various protocols sit within that model”; “differentiate between common networking protocols”).
CIS 260 (Y1 Spring) — Foundations of Relational Databases Boundary becomes a data/persistence boundary: schema as contract; transaction as all-or-nothing contract. Transaction content is a proposed addition, not yet in CIS 260’s confirmed content — see the “Proposed Changes” section of cis-260.md (same gap already logged from the Computational Models review).
CIS 251 (Y2 Fall) — Foundations of Cybersecurity A trust boundary is a contract about what each side may assume — the formalized pass.
CIS 301 / CIS 400 (Y2 Fall / Y2 Spring) Contracts meet verification and testing: CIS 301 reasons formally about whether a contract holds; CIS 400 checks it empirically (testing, formalized).
CIS 400 (Y2 Spring) — capstone of the core Defend the boundaries and contracts you designed — the course’s live-walkthrough/architecture-defense assessment. Contracts over time: API/semantic versioning as a compatibility contract, closing the loop opened by semver’s introduction in CIS 116’s professional-practices touch. CIS 400’s own “Software Versioning” topic confirms its half of this claim; CIS 116’s semver introduction is unconfirmed (Topics/Assignments still TBD). The live-walkthrough/architecture-defense assessment claim is confirmed real design intent but not yet in the drafted course page (2026-08-03) — see the “Confirmed Design Intent” section added to cis-400.md.
