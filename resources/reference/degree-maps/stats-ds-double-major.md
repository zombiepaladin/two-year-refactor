# CS + Statistics/Data Science Double Major — Overlap Analysis

Checks whether the two-year CS core (`cs.md`) combined with the Statistics department's B.S. in Statistics and Data Science (`stats-ds.md`) could be completed as a double major without meaningful extra credit hours beyond either degree alone. Short answer: **no — there's a genuine, substantial gap of roughly 30 credit hours**, driven almost entirely by content Statistics requires that has no CS equivalent, not by anything fixable through better course selection. The overlap that does exist is real and large, though, and worth using deliberately.

**Total additional credits: ~32** (1+3+3+6+3+3+8+2+3), on top of the CS map's own 121 — landing a realistic combined double-major total around **153 credit hours**, not 121 and not the naive 241 you'd get by summing two fully separate degrees.

## Why the gap is real, not a sequencing artifact

The additional 32 credits aren't spread thin across many small frictions — they cluster almost entirely in one place: **Statistics's own specialized sequence** (STAT 150, Intro Stats I & II, Mathematical Statistics I & II, Design Methods, Linear Models, Statistics Computing — 24 of the 32 credits) plus **the two extra semesters of calculus** (8 credits) that Statistics requires and Computer Science simply doesn't. Neither of these is a course-selection or scheduling problem — they're content a Statistics degree considers foundational that a CS core was never built to include, and vice versa isn't true (nothing CS-specific is missing from the Statistics side once the overlaps above are used).

**One real uncertainty, not resolved here**: whether the CS core's own `STAT 410` (Y2S2, 4 credits, "new stats course developed to support the two-year core") overlaps at all with STAT/DS's Introductory Statistics I/II or Mathematical Statistics sequence. If `STAT 410` turns out to be pedagogically close to STAT 225/240/250 (an applied intro stats course), it could offset 3 of the 32 additional credits. It almost certainly does *not* substitute for STAT 610/611 (Mathematical Statistics), which is calculus-based and proof-oriented in a way a CS-service stats course isn't designed to be. This wasn't checked against `STAT 410`'s actual content because that content doesn't exist yet at block-file level — worth revisiting once it does.

## Bottom line

Roughly 90 credit hours of the two degrees' combined 120+120 genuinely overlap (all of K-State Core, Outside Concentration, Overlays, Linear Algebra, Programming Foundations, Advanced Writing, and 6 of 9 upper-division-elective credits) — that's real and substantial, and makes this double major meaningfully more efficient than doing the two degrees separately. But it is not free: **the double major realistically costs about 32 credit hours beyond the CS degree alone**, almost entirely because Statistics's own mathematical-statistics core and its full calculus sequence have no counterpart in the CS map and aren't something the CS side can reasonably absorb without becoming a different degree. "Without extra hours" isn't achievable as stated; "with substantially less than double the hours" is.
