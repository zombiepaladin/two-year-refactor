## Organizing principle: systems thinking as the program-wide notional machine

Every thread in this document is an instance of one through-line: building a notional machine robust enough that students can reason about a system they didn't build. "System" is read at three scopes, and every concept thread should be tagged with which scope(s) it addresses:

- **artifact-internal** — execution/state model of the computational artifact itself (memory, call stack, concurrency, abstraction cost)
- **human-interaction** — the mental model a *user* forms of the artifact, and whether the artifact accommodates the range of users forming it (connects directly to UDL, `curriculum-design-references.md` §3)
- **embedding-systems** — the organizational/social/infrastructural context the artifact sits in (maintenance, dependencies, second-order effects, professional/ethical stakes)

This principle is grounded in Doll's postmodern curriculum theory (`curriculum-design-references.md` §7): Relation (Doll) is the direct antecedent of the three-scope notional machine (pedagogical relation ≈ artifact-internal; cultural/social relation ≈ human-interaction and embedding-systems). Recursion (Doll) is a stronger test than Bruner's spiral alone — a genuine spiral revisit should *transform* a student's earlier understanding, not just add to it. Rigor (Doll) is the standing check against richness/relation/recursion producing an open but incoherent design: when in doubt whether a pairing or thread earns its place, that's the question to ask.

## Professional practice & sociotechnical re-homing (2026-07-13)

**This isn't a new design problem — v1 already solved it.** `content/core-design/lenses/lens-professional-practices.md` and `content/core-design/threads/thread-sociotechnical.md` (both live, unarchived, carried forward as idea-bank per the earlier "keep, don't archive" decision) split this exact concern into two deliberately separate tracks:

### 1. APIs & Networked Systems (v1 spiral) — candidate for retirement/folding into the `networked-computing` norm

v1 treats this as a discrete 4-pass spiral: consume (B2, CS-106) → build (B4, CS-111) → integrate (B7, CS-208) → operate (B8, CS-210). The new design makes networked-computing a **cross-cutting norm** paired to concept-introduction points from CS-101 day one (see the pairing table above and `cs-101-seed-sequence.md`), not a discrete thread confined to a B2–B8 arc. Keeping both as-is would produce a direct contradiction: the old spiral's "first pass = consume, at B2" vs. the new norm's "network-boundary reasoning starts at CS-101, B1." Two ways to resolve, neither decided here:
- Retire the spiral entirely; fold its four verbs (consume/build/integrate/operate) into the norm's pairing table as an escalation-of-capability axis, distinct from "when is the norm first paired."
- Keep the spiral as the capability-escalation tracker, and let the norm own only the *timing/pairing* question (which concept-introduction moments get a networking pairing), explicitly reframing the spiral's first pass to acknowledge the earlier CS-101 seed rather than claim B2 as the true first touch.

**Competency/ABET flag:** this thread doesn't carry its own competency ID (threads aren't competencies), but the courses that lean on it for competency evidence (CS-111 Systems & APIs, CS-208 Data Integration, CS-209 OSI Networking) map to SI.1 (Analyze System Behavior) and DI.2 (Manage Data Resources) partly *because* this thread's spiral existed to justify the escalation. `resources/reference/abet/cs.md` line 112 explicitly requires "exposure to... networking and communication" as part of the CS ABET criteria — whichever resolution is chosen, the networking-exposure claim needs to still be traceable somewhere legible to an ABET reviewer, not just implicit in scattered norm-pairing rows. Flag for whoever does the ABET mapping pass.

### 2. Computational Models (v1 flagship spiral) — candidate for **partial** refactor, not full retirement

This thread currently carries concurrency as one facet of its model taxonomy (imperative/functional/declarative/concurrent-reactive/dataflow/logic-constraint), with a full B1→B2→B4→B8 concurrency arc woven through its own rows (CS-101's goroutine-inspired seed, CS-106's event-loop, CS-111's dataflow, CS-210's OS-formalization/actor-model). The new design moves concurrency to a cross-cutting **norm**, paired at introduction points across many threads rather than living inside one flagship thread's own escalation. Recommend:
- **Do not retire the thread wholesale.** The non-concurrency taxonomy work (imperative/functional/declarative/logic-constraint/dataflow as a landscape of ways to express computation) is real, distinctive content with no equivalent in the new norms document, and nothing proposes replacing it.
- **Do** re-home the concurrency-specific rows (the B1/B2/B4/B8 concurrency material currently inside this thread) so they're owned by the `concurrency` norm's pairing table instead of duplicated in both places. This is a de-duplication move, not a content cut.

**ABET flag, explicit and load-bearing:** this thread's own "Connections" section states plainly: "this also supplies the ABET operating-systems exposure." `resources/reference/abet/cs.md` line 112 requires exposure to "operating systems, and parallel and distributed computing" — currently anchored entirely on this thread's B1 (CS-101/CS-103 process/concurrency seeds) and B8 (CS-210 OS formalization) rows. If concurrency content moves to the norm and this thread's B1/B8 rows are stripped without the norm's own documentation picking up the explicit "this is our OS/parallel-computing ABET exposure" statement, **the ABET claim could silently lose its documented anchor.** This needs to be carried over explicitly, not assumed to survive the refactor.

**Competency flag:** CS-101/CS-102's existing competency mappings for the concurrency seed (CR.1, CR.2 — see both files' Competency Assessments sections) are tied to specific artifacts (the H5P multi-voice widget, the shared-memory hand-trace). If the norm-pairing redesign changes what artifact carries the concurrency content at CS-101, these mappings need re-verification, not silent carry-over.
