A.I. Systems

Working draft for faculty review.

Premise

The B.S. in Artificial Intelligence shares its first two years in full with the B.S. in Computer Science — the same core, course for course. The upper division is where the two degrees diverge: A.I. adds a dedicated sequence covering AI foundations, machine learning, knowledge engineering, and an applied domain, built primarily from K-State’s existing undergraduate CIS course suite together with a small number of new courses proposed alongside this degree.

Because an A.I. student completes the entire Computer Science core plus a full A.I. specialization, this degree map is built to independently satisfy both ABET’s Computer Science criteria and its Artificial Intelligence criteria.

The degree includes two capstone experiences with distinct purposes: CIS 400, taken at the midpoint of the program, is a substantial team-based systems project that validates the two-year core before a student moves into specialization coursework. CIS 597 (AI Capstone), taken in the final semester, is the specialization’s own major project.

ABET Artificial Intelligence Requirement Fulfillment

ABET’s Artificial Intelligence criteria require at least 42 semester credit hours of AI coursework covering seven fundamental topics plus advanced AI topics, at least one AI application area, and a major project — and separately, at least 9 semester credit hours of mathematics and statistics covering statistical inference and modeling, linear algebra, probability, data visualization, and optimization.

Fundamental AI topics

AI foundations — reasoning, heuristic search, knowledge representation CIS 530, CIS 635
Programming, data structures, and algorithms The shared core (CIS 116, 200, 300, and surrounding coursework)

Machine learning, including deep learning CIS 532
Design and implementation of AI solutions Project work in CIS 530, 532, 537, and 635, culminating in CIS 597
AI system architecture and infrastructure See faculty concerns below
Ethics and responsible AI CIS 415, together with the core’s disciplined-AI-use practice

Advanced AI topics: CIS 535 (Neuro-Symbolic AI) and CIS 534 (Information Retrieval and Text Mining) build directly on the fundamentals above to provide depth.

Application area: Computer Vision (CIS 537).

Major project: CIS 597 (AI Capstone).

AI coursework credit hours: the CIS-prefixed coursework in this map (shared core plus specialization) totals well over 50 semester credit hours, clearing the 42-hour floor with room to spare.

Mathematics and statistics: MATH 515 or 551 (linear algebra, 3 credits) and STAT 410 (statistical inference, probability, and data visualization, 4 credits) together provide 7 of the required 9 semester credit hours. Optimization coverage is not yet confirmed — see faculty concerns below.

ABET Computer Science Requirement Fulfillment

ABET’s Computer Science criteria require at least 30 semester credit hours of computing coursework (including techniques and tools for computing practice, security and privacy principles, and the societal impacts of computing), of which at least 40 semester credit hours must be CS-specific — covering algorithms and complexity, computer science theory, programming language concepts, and software development in substantial depth; substantial depth in at least one general-purpose language; exposure to computer architecture, information management, networking, operating systems, and parallel and distributed computing; the study of computing systems at varying levels of abstraction; and a major project. Separately, at least 15 semester credit hours of mathematics and statistics are required, including discrete mathematics, probability, and statistics, at a rigor at least equivalent to introductory calculus.

Algorithms, theory, languages, and software development: CIS 300 and CIS 575 (algorithms and complexity); CIS 115, CIS 301, CIS 505, and CIS 220/225 (computer science theory — computability, formal languages, automata); CIS 505 (programming language concepts); CIS 400 and CIS 501 (software development).

General-purpose language: CIS 116 and CIS 200/300 provide sustained, substantial work in a single general-purpose language across three courses.

Exposure areas: computer architecture (CIS 115); information management (CIS 260, CIS 560); networking (CIS 225); operating systems and parallel/distributed computing — corrected 2026-08-04: CIS 220 gives a light, genuine OS-exposure touch (“browser as mini-OS”), but the deeper formalization this row used to attribute to CIS 220/CIS 300 core content was never actually built there (see resources/reference/spiral-threads.md’s correction note). This map already requires the Systems Elective (CIS 520, 525, or 625, Year 4 Semester 1) — that’s the real, confirmed source of this coverage, not the core courses.

Abstraction across scales: the core’s progression from representation (CIS 115) through data structures (CIS 300) to systems and architecture (CIS 450, CIS 501) demonstrates computing systems at multiple levels of abstraction.

Major project: satisfied jointly by CIS 400 (core-level systems project) and CIS 597 (specialization capstone).

Mathematics: MATH 220 (calculus), the discrete-math sequence, STAT 410, and the Year 2 math elective (MATH 515 or 551) together total 15 semester credit hours, meeting the floor exactly.

Security, privacy, and societal impact (general-criteria requirement, not CS-specific): CIS 251 and CIS 415.

Concerns for Faculty

  1. Four courses in this map do not yet exist in the catalog: CIS 534, 535, 536, and 597. This degree cannot be offered as designed until they are formally proposed and approved. CIS 536 is currently running as a pilot under generic special-topics numbers (CIS 590/890); CIS 534 and 535 are proposed as undergraduate counterparts to existing graduate courses; CIS 597 is an entirely new capstone.
  2. CIS 534’s number reflects a resolved catalog conflict. The natural undergraduate counterpart number for its graduate parent course (CIS 833) would have been 533, but that number is already assigned to Introduction to Data Science Foundations. 534 is used instead.
  3. AI system architecture and infrastructure — model serving, ML pipelines, scaling, vector stores — has no course coverage in this map. This is a real gap against the ABET fundamental topic, not addressed by any existing or proposed course. It should be treated as a genuine open need, not assumed to be covered by adjacent coursework.
  4. Optimization coverage toward the 9-hour math/statistics floor is unconfirmed. Linear algebra and statistics account for 7 of the 9 required hours; an optimization course is needed to close the gap, and none has been identified and confirmed at the undergraduate level.
  5. CIS 415’s sufficiency for the ABET “ethics and responsible AI” topic should be confirmed explicitly, rather than assumed from its pairing with the core’s general AI-use practices.
  6. Total program length is 121 credit hours, one hour above a typical 120-hour target. This matches the Computer Science degree’s own total under this design and is not specific to this specialization.
